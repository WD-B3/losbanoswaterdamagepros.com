Musty Smell After Water Damage in Los Banos: What It Means

A musty odor after a leak is a clue that something is still damp. Finding it matters more than covering it.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

A musty smell is one of the most common complaints after a leak, and one of the most misunderstood. People spray, light candles and run fans, and the smell returns. That happens because the smell is a symptom. Somewhere in the house, a material is still damp, and the damp is the problem.

This post explains where musty smells come from after water damage, how to track them down and what actually removes them. It does not make health claims. For health questions, talk to a physician.

Why a damp smell lingers

Water soaks into porous materials such as drywall, insulation, carpet pad, wood and cardboard. If those materials stay wet, microbial growth can begin, and growth is what produces a stale, earthy odor. The 24 to 48 hour window the EPA gives for drying water-damaged areas and items is the practical clock here to prevent mold growth, and it states that controlling moisture is the key to controlling mold.

A smell that appears days or weeks after a leak means moisture was left behind. The surface may feel dry while the material underneath is not.

Where to look first

Under flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and the subfloor beneath can stay wet long after the top looks dry.

Inside walls

Insulation and the back of drywall hold moisture and dry slowly without airflow.

Cabinets and closets

Closed spaces trap humid air, and swollen cabinet bases hide leaks.

Crawl spaces and attics

Out-of-sight spaces collect moisture from leaks, vents and poor ventilation.

How to find the source

  1. 1

    Follow your nose

    Walk the house and note where the smell is strongest, especially near the original leak.

  2. 2

    Check for stains and soft spots

    Look for discoloration, bubbling paint, warped baseboards and soft drywall.

  3. 3

    Lift a corner

    Carpet can be lifted at an edge to check the pad and tack strip for dampness.

  4. 4

    Use a moisture meter

    A meter reads moisture inside materials that look dry. Crews use thermal cameras too.

If you find the damp area, do not paint over it. Painting seals moisture in and makes the problem harder to find later.

What actually removes the smell

ApproachDoes it work?Why
Air freshener or candlesNoIt covers the odor but leaves the damp material in place
Running a fan aloneRarelyIt moves air but may not pull moisture from deep materials
Drying with air movers and dehumidifiersYes, if readings reach a dry levelIt removes the moisture that feeds the odor
Removing materials that cannot dryYesSoft drywall, pad and insulation are removed and replaced

After materials are dry or removed, surfaces can be cleaned. Odor that remains after drying often comes from a material that should have been removed, such as a pad or insulation. A crew can tell you which.

Humidity and everyday habits

Even without a leak, a closed house can build humidity. Run bathroom fans, use the kitchen vent when cooking and keep furniture a few inches away from exterior walls. A small hygrometer from a hardware store shows indoor humidity. Readings that stay high suggest a moisture source worth tracing.

Do not run a humidifier in a room you are trying to dry. Keep clothes and towels from piling up damp in closets and laundry areas.

When the smell comes from the HVAC system

Sometimes the odor travels through the ducts. A clogged condensate line, a wet drain pan or damp insulation around the air handler can put a stale smell into every room that the system serves. If the smell is strongest at vents, or it appears only when the system runs, the source may be in the equipment.

Turn the system off if you see water near the air handler, and have an HVAC technician inspect the drain, the pan and the float switch. The AC leak page explains how condensate water reaches ceilings and walls.

What a professional check adds

A moisture check does not rely on smell. A crew compares readings in the suspect area with readings from a similar dry area and maps where they differ. A thermal camera shows cooler, wetter zones behind surfaces. Together they show whether the smell comes from a small damp area or a wide one.

With a map in hand, only the wet materials are opened, dried or removed. That keeps repair work small and gives you readings to share with an insurer or a landlord.

How to tell if the problem is fixed

After drying, the smell should fade over days, not return. If it comes back in warm or humid weather, moisture may still be present in a place that was missed. Take new readings in the same spots. A dry reading in the same spots that were wet before is the best sign that the fix worked.

Damp smells in Los Banos homes

Los Banos has hot dry summers and wet winters, and the swing between them affects how homes hold moisture. A house closed up for cooling can trap humid air, and a house that gets a winter leak can stay damp through the cool months. Tell the crew when the leak happened and when the smell started, since the timeline helps them find it.

Rental tenants should report a persistent damp smell to the landlord in writing and keep a dated photo of any stain. The owner may need to open a wall or lift flooring to find the source.

Common questions

Does a musty smell always mean mold?

Not always, but it means moisture is likely present, and moisture is what allows mold to grow. A moisture check finds the source.

Can I just air out the house?

Airing out helps with surface humidity but rarely dries materials inside walls or under floors. Readings tell you whether more is needed.

How long after a leak can the smell start?

It can start within days if materials stay wet. That is why drying within 24 to 48 hours matters.
