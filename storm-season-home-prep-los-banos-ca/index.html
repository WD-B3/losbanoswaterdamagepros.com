Storm Season Home Prep for Los Banos Homeowners

A few hours of checking the roof, gutters and drainage before the rain can prevent a night of buckets and towels.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

Winter storms are the main threat to buildings in the Los Banos area, and they arrive on their own schedule. In January 2023, severe storms flooded parts of Merced County, led to evacuations in Planada and opened sandbag pickup points in cities that included Los Banos and Dos Palos. Preparation will not stop a storm, but it can limit what water does when it arrives.

This checklist is for homeowners and renters. Do the outside items on a dry day, and do not climb onto a wet roof.

Outside the house

  • Clear gutters and downspouts of leaves and debris.
  • Make sure downspouts discharge away from the foundation.
  • Look at the roof from the ground for missing or lifted shingles and cracked flashing.
  • Trim branches that hang over the roof.
  • Check that the soil near the foundation slopes away from the house.
  • Clear storm drains near your property of leaves, trash and debris.

Do not climb a roof to check it. Use binoculars from the ground, or ask a roofer to inspect. A small repair before the rain costs less than the interior damage after it.

Inside the house

Windows and doors

Check seals and weatherstripping. Wind-driven rain finds gaps.

Ceilings

Look for stains from earlier leaks. They show where water has entered before.

Attic

If you can reach it safely, look for daylight, wet insulation and damp wood.

Water heater and shutoffs

Know where the main valve and the water heater shutoff are.

Take photos of your home and valuables before the season. A dated record makes a claim easier if damage happens. Store copies in a cloud folder or send them to yourself.

When the storm hits

If water starts coming in, stay safe first. Move belongings away from leaks, place buckets and keep away from rooms with sagging ceilings. Do not wade through water near outlets or cords. If a power line is down, call the utility or 911.

After the rain, photograph the damage and call for help. Wet insulation and carpet should be dealt with quickly, because the EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours.

Know your flood risk

The FEMA flood map for your address shows whether it lies in a mapped flood zone. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, according to the California Department of Insurance, and flood coverage is a separate purchase. Flood policies often have a waiting period before they take effect, so the time to ask is before the storm.

A short plan for the household

Decide before the storm who does what. One person can watch the roof and ceilings, another can handle buckets and towels, and someone should know where the main valve and the electrical panel are. Write the plan on a card and keep it with the emergency numbers.

If anyone in the household depends on powered medical equipment, plan for a power outage and keep the utility's contact information on hand. Check on neighbors who may need help.

Protecting belongings

  • Store important papers in a waterproof container and keep them above floor level.
  • Lift boxes in the garage onto shelves or pallets.
  • Move electronics away from windows and exterior walls.
  • Keep a small bag with medicines, chargers and copies of IDs.
  • Park vehicles on higher ground if street flooding is likely.

A few minutes of lifting can prevent the loss of items that cannot be replaced. Cardboard boxes on a concrete floor wick water fast, so move them first.

After the storm: a quick walk-through

Once the rain stops and it is safe, walk through the house. Look at ceilings for stains or sagging, press on the carpet near exterior doors, and check the walls under windows. Look in the attic if you can do so safely. Photograph anything wet, with the date on your phone.

If you find water, start drying right away. Remove wet items, run fans, and call for help if more than a small area is wet. The sooner the drying starts, the more of the house can be saved.

Storm resources around Los Banos

Merced County posts storm and sandbag information through its Office of Emergency Services, and the City of Los Banos shares updates on its website. Check both before a forecast storm. If you live in Dos Palos or Newman, check your own city's page as well.

Los Banos sits near the San Luis National Wildlife Refuge Complex and the Grassland Ecological Area, and the city takes its name from Los Banos Creek. Low ground and drainage matter, so look at where water collects near your property in a heavy rain.

Common questions

When should I prepare for storm season in Los Banos?

Do the outside checks before the rainy season starts, on a dry day. Do not wait for a forecast storm to find a clogged gutter.

Where do I get sandbags?

Merced County and local cities have opened pickup points before storms. Check the county and city websites for current locations.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm flooding?

Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof is often covered, while rising flood water is typically excluded. Ask your insurer which applies.
