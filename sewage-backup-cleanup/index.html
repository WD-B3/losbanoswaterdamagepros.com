Sewage backup is a health hazard, and it is an emergency whenever it happens. This page covers sewage backup cleanup in Los Banos, the 24/7 job of removing contaminated water, discarding what cannot be cleaned and sanitizing the area.

Stay out of the water, keep children and pets away, and do not run water or flush toilets, since that adds to the backup. Open windows for air if it is safe, and call the line.

Toilets gurgling, drains backing up, a sewage smell in the house?

Most people do not search for Category 3 water. They search for a toilet that will not stop gurgling, a floor drain bubbling up, sewage coming out of a shower, or a smell that will not go away. Those are signs of a blockage or line problem, and the trade term for the cleanup is sewage backup cleanup.

Category 3 water is the most contaminated class in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. It is handled with containment, protective equipment and disposal rather than ordinary drying.

Sewage backup emergency: safety first

Do not handle sewage yourself

Avoid touching the water, do not use a household wet vacuum on it, and keep food and dishes away from the area. The EPA advises calling a professional experienced with contaminated water when sewage or other contaminated water caused the damage. If you smell gas or the water is near electrical items, call the utility or 911 first.

A plumber or the city identifies where the blockage is. If it is in the city main, the City of Los Banos Public Works department should be told. If it is in your own lateral line, a plumber clears it, and then cleanup can finish.

How sewage cleanup is done

    The area is isolated so contamination does not spread through the home.

Sewer lines in Los Banos

Sewer service in Los Banos is run by the city, and a backup that comes from the main line is a Public Works matter. If several neighbors have the same problem, call Public Works and tell them. If only your home is affected, the blockage is more likely in your own lateral line, which a plumber can inspect with a camera.

Heavy storm runoff can also overwhelm drainage. In January 2023, severe storms across Merced County led to flooding and sandbag distribution in several cities, a reminder to ask your insurer about sewer backup coverage, which is often a separate endorsement.

What to do while you wait for help

  • Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the area.
  • Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washing machine so the backup does not grow.
  • Turn off the HVAC system if the air handler or ducts are near the contaminated area.
  • Take photos of the water, the affected rooms and damaged belongings.
  • Put on rubber boots and gloves only if you must enter, and wash well afterward.

These steps limit the spread while a crew and, if needed, a plumber arrive. They do not replace professional cleanup, since the hidden contamination in porous materials is the part household products cannot reach.

Common questions

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Los Banos?

Yes. Sewage backups are a health and safety emergency at any hour, so someone picks up whatever the hour, weekends and holidays included. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available. Keep people and pets out of the area and avoid contact with the water.

Is sewage water dangerous to touch?

Yes. Sewage can carry bacteria and other contaminants, so avoid contact and call a professional experienced with contaminated water.

Will you throw out my carpet?

Usually yes. Carpet and pad that touched sewage are commonly removed, because porous materials cannot be reliably sanitized.

Who do I call first, the city or you?

If the blockage may be in the city main, tell Public Works. For the cleanup and safety, call us. If it is your own line, a plumber clears it and cleanup can proceed.
