Downtown Los Banos is the older center of the city, and its edges are not a mystery. The City's Downtown Master Plan draws its study area from H Street on the north to Pacheco Boulevard (State Route 152) on the south, and from Fourth Street on the west to Seventh Street on the east. Henry Miller Plaza is the main civic space.

This page is for people who live on the residential blocks of that area. Water damage in older housing behaves differently from water damage in a newer tract, so the guidance below is written for that setting. Describe your situation to us at (831) 271-0459 and we will start a plan with you.

What the city says about the area

The Master Plan says the east-west lettered streets lead to residential homes and apartments that are part of Downtown but outside the commercial core. It also describes the architecture as a mix of classical and Art Deco buildings, with some Spanish Revival examples. One local housing guide describes early 1900s homes on grid-style blocks.

What that means for a leak is practical. Older homes can hold plumbing and wall materials that differ from what a newer house has. We cannot know what is behind your wall until we look, so we measure moisture before we decide what to open.

Storms, drains and sewer lines downtown

The Master Plan lists a storm drain network of about 4,926 linear feet inside the study area. Its wastewater summary also notes that existing storm drain connections cause sudden increases in wastewater flow during a storm event. For a homeowner, the lesson is to treat a slow drain or a gurgling fixture during heavy rain as a warning sign.

If a floor drain or toilet backs up, do not clean it yourself. Sewage is contaminated water and needs trained handling, which is covered on our sewage backup cleanup page. Keep children and pets out of the room and call us.

Before help arrives

Do not touch breakers or switches while standing in water. If the panel is in a wet room, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Close the main water valve if you can reach it safely. Lift small items off wet floors and take photos of the damage and the source.

How we dry a downtown home

  1. 1

    Find the source

    We trace the leak or backup and stop it where we can, so drying is not working against new water.

  2. 2

    Remove standing water

    Pumps and extractors pull water from floors and carpet. See water extraction.

  3. 3

    Dry the structure

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry wall cavities and subfloors. See structural drying.

  4. 4

    Check the walls

    Wet plaster, drywall and trim are checked with meters. See wall and drywall repair.

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours. In an older house, that window matters because wet wood and plaster dry slowly when the air is still.

Insurance paperwork for a downtown loss

The California Department of Insurance explains that flood damage typically needs its own policy, while a sudden pipe or appliance failure is a question for your homeowners insurer. Renters should also read the tenants and landlords guide. Coverage is decided by your insurer.

We keep photos, moisture readings and a written scope that you can hand to the adjuster. Save receipts and any failed part. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check an address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center if flood status matters to you.

Common questions

Where is Downtown Los Banos?

The City of Los Banos Downtown Master Plan sets its study area at H Street on the north, Pacheco Boulevard on the south, Fourth Street on the west and Seventh Street on the east.

Do you work on apartments as well as houses downtown?

Yes. Call and describe the unit, the source and how far the water has spread. If you rent, tell your landlord as well.

What should I do if a toilet backs up during a storm?

Stay out of the water, keep people and pets away and call. Sewage is contaminated and should not be cleaned up as a do-it-yourself job.

Is my home in a flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. You can look up an address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

How quickly should water be dried?

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to help prevent mold growth.
