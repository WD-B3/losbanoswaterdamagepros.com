Sudden Water Bill Jump in Los Banos? Check for a Hidden Leak

A bill that rises with no change in habits is often the first sign of water escaping somewhere you cannot see.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

Most people notice a water leak when a stain appears. A better early warning is the bill. If your usage jumps without a change in household habits, water may be escaping from a pipe under the slab, inside a wall or from a toilet that does not stop running.

This guide gives you a simple way to test for a leak, a list of the usual suspects and a plan for what to do next. It is written for Los Banos homeowners and renters, but the method works anywhere.

Step 1: Compare the bills

Look at the last several bills and compare the usage numbers, not just the dollar amounts. Seasonal changes are normal, since summer irrigation and cooling raise use. A jump during a cool month, or a jump that does not match any change at home, is the signal to dig further.

Check for visitors, a new appliance or a pool fill before you assume a leak. Those explain many sudden increases.

Step 2: The meter test

  1. 1

    Turn everything off

    Close taps, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure nobody flushes.

  2. 2

    Find the meter

    It is usually near the street in a box with a lid. Lift the lid carefully.

  3. 3

    Read the dial

    Many meters have a small low-flow indicator that spins when water moves.

  4. 4

    Wait and recheck

    After 15 to 30 minutes, check again. Movement with everything off suggests a leak.

If the dial moves, close the main valve at the house and check again. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

Step 3: The usual suspects

Toilets

A worn flapper lets water seep into the bowl. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait to see if color reaches the bowl.

Irrigation

A broken sprinkler head or a stuck valve can run for hours without anyone noticing.

Water heater

Check the base of the tank and the fittings for dampness or rust.

Hidden lines

A pipe in a slab or wall can leak with no puddle. Warm patches on floors and damp baseboards are clues.

Step 4: Signs of a hidden leak

  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • Warm spots on a slab floor.
  • Damp carpet along one wall or musty smell in a closet.
  • Cracks or stains that appear and grow.
  • Low pressure at fixtures that was not there before.

Quick checks you can do in ten minutes

  • Walk the house and listen for running water or hissing.
  • Look under every sink for drips or damp cabinet floors.
  • Check the washing machine hoses and the area behind the refrigerator.
  • Look at the base of the water heater and the pressure relief pipe.
  • Walk the yard and look for soggy ground, a wet spot or unusually green grass near a line.

These checks catch the obvious causes. If everything looks normal and the meter still moves, the leak is hidden. At that point a plumber with detection tools is the right call, and restoration comes after the pipe is found.

What a hidden leak can damage

Water from a hidden leak soaks flooring, baseboards, drywall and insulation, and it can travel along framing to places far from the source. Over time it can lift tile, warp wood floors and leave a musty smell. The longer it runs, the more material is wet and the more drying is needed.

That is why a small bill increase is worth investigating. Catching a leak early usually means a smaller job, a smaller bill and fewer materials to replace.

After you find the leak

Once the pipe is repaired, the damage question remains. Water that has been escaping for days or weeks may have soaked the subfloor, the baseboards and the bottom of walls. Ask for moisture readings before anyone closes up a wall or lays new flooring. Closing wet materials in is the usual way a plumbing repair turns into a mold and odor problem.

Keep the plumber's invoice and the dated bills. They show when the leak was found and fixed, and an insurer may ask for them.

Utility bills and leaks in Los Banos

The City of Los Banos bills for water, and the account shows usage by period. Keep the bills, since they are useful evidence of when usage started to climb. If you rent, send the numbers to your landlord in writing with the date you ran the meter test.

Some cities offer a one-time review of a leak adjustment. Ask the city's billing office whether Los Banos has a leak adjustment policy, because the answer can change what you do next.

What to do if you find a leak

Close the main valve and call a plumber to locate and fix the line. If water has already reached floors or walls, call for extraction and drying, since moisture left in place causes odor and mold. A hidden leak under a slab is covered on the slab leak page, and a burst line is covered on the burst pipe page.

Tell your insurer, especially if you find damage. Insurers often treat sudden leaks differently from long-term seepage, which is why a dated record of the bill jump and your meter test helps. Common questions

Why did my water bill double?

Common causes include a running toilet, a broken sprinkler line, a hidden pipe leak or a change in household use. The meter test helps tell them apart.

Can a leak under the slab raise my bill?

Yes. Water escaping under the slab is a classic cause of a bill that climbs with no visible damage.

Who pays for a leak adjustment on my bill in Los Banos?

Ask the City of Los Banos billing office whether a leak adjustment is available. Policies vary, and the city decides.
