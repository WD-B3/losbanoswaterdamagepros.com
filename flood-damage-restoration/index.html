Flood damage is different from a plumbing leak because the water comes from outside and it is rarely clean. Creek overflow, groundwater and storm runoff all carry soil, bacteria and debris into a home. This page covers flood damage cleanup in Los Banos, the 24/7 job of removing that water and restoring what can be saved.

If water is entering the house, get people and pets out of it, stay away from outlets and appliances, and do not wade through the water to move belongings. Call the line and describe the depth, how it got in, and which rooms are affected.

Floodwater emergency: contamination and safety

Floodwater is generally treated as Category 2 or 3, meaning it can contain contaminants and is not safe to handle with bare hands or mix with household cleaning. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference for water damage restoration categories, and a company can tell you whether its crews work to it.

Porous materials that sat in floodwater, such as carpet pad, insulation and some drywall, are usually removed rather than dried. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned, treated and dried.

Before you touch anything

Do not turn on electrical items that stood in water. If the water reached outlets or the panel, call the utility or 911 about the risk before calling the restoration line. Keep children and pets out of the water, and wear boots and gloves if you must cross it.

Common questions

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Los Banos?

Yes. Floods rarely arrive during business hours, so the phone is staffed overnight and all weekend. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the dispatcher will ask about water depth, source and any electrical hazards first.

Is floodwater always contaminated?

It should be treated that way. Floodwater from outside usually carries soil and bacteria, and it is handled as Category 2 or 3 unless a professional determines otherwise.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Typically not. The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies exclude flood, so check your policy and ask about a separate flood policy.

Can I save my carpet after a flood?

Usually not if the water was contaminated. Carpet and pad that sat in floodwater are commonly removed, and hard flooring may be saved after cleaning and drying.
