What water damage restoration includes

Restoration is a sequence, and each step matters. First the source is stopped. Then standing water is removed with pumps and extraction units, wet materials are sorted into what can dry and what has to go, and drying equipment is set. Moisture readings are taken until the structure reaches a dry standard. Repairs, such as new drywall, flooring or paint, come last.

Within 24 to 48 hours is the EPA's target for drying water-damaged areas and items to prevent mold growth. That window is why a prompt call matters more than a perfect plan. Industry practice follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sorts water into three categories and sets how each is handled.

When to treat a leak as an emergency

Water standing on the floor, a ceiling that is sagging or dripping, a pipe that has burst and a sewage backup are all emergencies. So is a water heater that is leaking steadily, a flooded room after a storm or a smell of damp that appeared with a stain. If you are unsure, call. The dispatcher will tell you whether the situation is urgent and what to shut off first.

Our 24/7 emergency line also handles questions that start with a surprise on a water bill or a damp spot on a wall. Hidden leaks cause much of the damage, and the earlier they are found, the smaller the job.

What water damage looks like in Los Banos homes

Los Banos sits in Merced County in the San Joaquin Valley, between the Coast Range and the wetlands of the San Luis National Wildlife Refuge Complex and the Grassland Ecological Area. The city takes its name from Los Banos Creek and was incorporated in 1907. Homes range from older properties near Henry Miller Plaza to newer subdivisions, and the plumbing that serves them varies just as much.

Losses here usually start in one of three places. The first is indoor plumbing, such as a washing machine hose, a water heater or a supply line under a sink. The second is the roof or windows during a winter storm. The third is a drain or sewer line that backs up. Each leads to a different cleanup, and each has its own page on this site.

Storms and the winter season

In January 2023, severe storms flooded parts of Merced County, with evacuations in Planada and sandbag pickup points in several cities, including Los Banos and Dos Palos. Storm water often arrives as Category 2 or 3 water, which changes what can be dried and what is removed. The storm water damage page covers roofs, windows and runoff, and the flood damage page covers rising water.

Before the rainy season, clear gutters, check the roof and look at where water drains away from the house. Our storm season guide walks through a simple checklist.

Water damage and your insurance

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and flood are often excluded. The California Department of Insurance notes that flood damage typically needs a separate flood policy. Ask your insurer which applies to your loss before work begins.
