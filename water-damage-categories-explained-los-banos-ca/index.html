Water Damage Categories Explained: Clean, Gray and Black Water

The category of water decides what can be dried, what must be removed and what safety steps are needed.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

Not all water damage is the same. A pipe that bursts and a sewer line that backs up both put water on the floor, but they call for different handling. The industry sorts water into three categories, and the category shapes everything from safety steps to what gets thrown away.

This guide explains the categories in plain language, using the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as the reference. It is general information, and a crew will assess your situation on site.

Category 1: Clean water

Category 1 water comes from a clean source, such as a broken supply line, a leaking faucet or a failed water heater tank. It does not pose a substantial risk when it first appears. Most materials can be dried, and the cleanup is the simplest of the three.

Clean water does not stay clean forever. Left to sit, it picks up contaminants from the materials it touches and can move to a higher category.

Category 2: Gray water

Category 2 water carries some contamination. Examples include discharge from a washing machine or dishwasher, an overflowing toilet that contains urine but no feces and water from a sump pump. It may cause discomfort if people come into contact with it.

Hard surfaces can usually be cleaned and dried. Porous materials such as carpet pad and soft furniture are often removed, depending on how long they were wet.

Category 3: Black water

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated. Sewage, floodwater from rivers or streams and standing water that has supported microbial growth fall here. It can carry harmful contaminants, so protective gear and containment are used.

Porous materials such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that touched it are generally removed and discarded. Hard materials are cleaned and sanitized. See the sewage backup page for the full process.

Why time changes the category

Water that sits in a building is exposed to warmth, dirt and materials that feed growth. After a day or two, clean water can become gray, and gray can become black. This is why prompt action matters even for a clean source.

The EPA recommends drying wet areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. The same window is a practical guide for keeping a loss from getting worse.

What you can do

  • Identify the source and stop it if safe.
  • Stay out of water you suspect is gray or black.
  • Wear gloves and boots if you must enter, and wash well after.
  • Do not use household vacuums on contaminated water.
  • Tell the dispatcher what you know about the source.

You do not have to classify the water yourself. A crew will assess it. Giving the source and the time helps them prepare.

Insurance and categories

The category can affect how a claim is handled. Sewage backup and flood are commonly excluded from standard homeowners policies unless coverage was added. The California Department of Insurance notes that flood damage typically needs a separate policy. Before you assume anything, read your policy and ask your agent about water backup and flood coverage.

Documentation matters more with contaminated water. Photos of the source, the date and the extent of the loss help your insurer understand what happened.

Common misunderstandings

  • Clean water is always safe. It is not, if it sits.
  • Bleach fixes everything. It does not dry materials or remove what is soaked in.
  • Black water means the house is lost. It does not. It means porous materials are removed and the structure is cleaned.
  • Rain is always clean water. It can pick up contaminants on the way in.

When in doubt, treat water as contaminated until a crew says otherwise, and keep people and pets out.

Common questions

What is the difference between gray and black water?

Gray water carries some contamination, such as appliance discharge. Black water is grossly contaminated, such as sewage or floodwater.

Can clean water become black water?

Yes. Left standing, water can pick up contaminants and microbial growth. Prompt action prevents that.

Is rainwater clean water?

Rain that enters through a roof is often treated as clean when it first arrives, but it can pick up contaminants from roofing and insulation. Runoff from the ground is not clean.
