How to Shut Off Your Home's Water Main in Los Banos

In a burst pipe emergency, the main valve is the first thing to close. Find it before you need it.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

Water damage help line
(831) 271-0459
Call Now

When a pipe bursts, every minute of running water adds to the damage. The fastest way to limit it is to close the main water valve. Many people do not know where theirs is until they need it, and by then the floor is wet.

This guide helps you find your valve, learn how it turns and test it, so that you can act in seconds. It applies to most homes in Los Banos, Dos Palos and Newman.

Where the main valve usually is

LocationWhat to look for
Near the water heaterA valve on the cold supply line, often at the house wall
Garage wallA valve where the supply enters, sometimes inside a small box
Utility room or closetA valve where the line comes through the foundation
Outside by a hose bibA valve on the supply near the foundation
At the meterA city shutoff near the street, in a box with a lid

Walk the perimeter of the house and look at where the water line enters. Follow the cold line from the water heater back to the wall. Label the valve with a tag once you find it.

Types of valves

Gate valve

Has a round handle. Turn it clockwise until it stops. These can stick or break if forced.

Ball valve

Has a lever handle. A quarter turn closes it, and the lever sits across the pipe when closed.

Meter shutoff

Belongs to the city and is usually not for homeowner use. Ask the city before using it.

Fixture shutoffs

Small valves under sinks and behind toilets. Turn them clockwise to close one fixture.

How to shut it off

  1. 1

    Find the valve

    Locate the main and clear anything blocking it.

  2. 2

    Turn it gently

    Turn clockwise for a gate valve, or a quarter turn for a ball valve.

  3. 3

    Open a faucet

    Open the lowest tap in the house to drain the line.

  4. 4

    Check the meter

    Confirm the dial has stopped. Then call for help.

If the valve will not turn, do not force it hard enough to break it. Call for help. A broken valve makes things worse. If the valve is stuck, you may need the city shutoff at the meter, and the City of Los Banos can tell you who handles it.

Test your valve once a year

Valves that are never used seize up. Once a year, turn yours off and on again. If it is stiff, a plumber can replace it before it fails in an emergency. After testing, check for drips at the stem and note how many turns it took.

What to do after the water is off

  • Open the lowest tap to drain the lines.
  • Turn off the water heater if the tank may drain.
  • Move belongings off wet floors and take photos.
  • Call your insurer and the 24/7 line.
  • Do not turn the water back on until a plumber has fixed the break.

Make a shutoff card for your household

Write down where the main valve is, which way it turns and where the water heater shutoff and the electrical panel are. Add the number of your insurer and the 24/7 line. Tape the card inside a kitchen cabinet and send a photo to everyone in the household.

In an emergency, people forget details. A card makes sure the person who is home, whether an adult, a teenager or a house sitter, can act without calling someone for directions.

Smart shutoff devices and leak sensors

Leak sensors are small devices that sound an alarm when water touches them. Place them under sinks, behind toilets, by the washing machine and near the water heater. Some models connect to automatic shutoff valves that close the main when a leak is detected.

These devices can help, but they are not a substitute for knowing where your manual valve is. Batteries die and devices fail. Test any device on a schedule and keep the manual valve accessible.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing a stuck valve until it breaks.
  • Closing the valve but forgetting to open a tap to drain the line.
  • Turning the water back on before the break has been fixed.
  • Blocking the valve with storage so it cannot be reached.
  • Assuming the meter valve is for homeowner use.

Avoiding these mistakes saves time and prevents a bad situation from getting worse. If you are not sure, call for help and describe what you see.

Water service in Los Banos

Homes in Los Banos receive water from the City of Los Banos, and the meter box near the street belongs to the city. The main valve at the house is yours to operate. If you cannot find it or it fails, call the city's public works line during business hours or the 24/7 line for restoration help.

Common questions

Which way do I turn the main valve?

A gate valve turns clockwise to close. A ball valve closes with a quarter turn so the lever sits across the pipe.

Is it safe to use the city shutoff at the meter?

It belongs to the city. Ask the City of Los Banos before using it, and use the house main when you can.

Should I turn off the water heater too?

If the tank may drain, yes. Turning off the heater prevents damage to the element. Follow the manufacturer instructions.
