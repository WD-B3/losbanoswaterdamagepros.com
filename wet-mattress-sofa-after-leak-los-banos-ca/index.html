Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak: Save It or Toss It?

Foam, batting and fabric hold water for a long time. What you can save depends on the source of the water and how fast you act.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

A leak does not only wet floors. Mattresses, sofas, recliners and rugs soak up water from below or from above, and they are expensive to replace. People often ask if a soaked piece of furniture can be saved, and the honest answer depends on two things: what kind of water it was and how long the furniture stayed wet.

This guide gives you a practical way to decide, along with the steps that give furniture its best chance.

Step 1: Know what kind of water it was

Water sourceCategoryFurniture outlook
Supply line, clean tap waterCategory 1Often can be dried and saved if acted on quickly
Washing machine or dishwasher dischargeCategory 2Hard surfaces may be cleaned, soft goods are risky
Sewage or floodwaterCategory 3Soft items are generally discarded
Clean water that sat for daysCan become worseOdor and growth may mean replacement

The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Clean water that is left to sit can change category over time, because bacteria and microbial growth build up. That is why the clock matters as much as the source.

Step 2: Get it out of the water

Lift the furniture off wet floors and carpet. Place foil, plastic or wood blocks under the legs so the wet floor does not stain the piece. Remove cushions and pillows, and separate them so air can reach every side. Move the furniture to a dry, ventilated room if you can, or outside on a dry day.

Take photos before you move anything. If you plan to claim the loss, the photos show the condition at the time.

Step 3: Dry what can be dried

Wood and metal frames

Wipe dry, then let air dry. Solid wood often recovers, but veneer can lift.

Fabric upholstery

Extract water if you can, then dry with fans and a dehumidifier. Foam inside dries slowly.

Leather

Wipe dry and let it air dry away from direct heat. It may shrink or stiffen.

Mattresses

Foam and innerspring mattresses hold water deep inside. Large soakings are rarely worth saving.

The EPA advises that wet items that cannot be cleaned and dried within 24 to 48 hours should be discarded, to prevent mold growth. Foam and thick padding are the hardest to dry in that time.

When to let it go

  • The water was sewage or floodwater.
  • The item stayed wet for more than a day or two and smells musty.
  • The mattress is soaked through, not just damp on the surface.
  • Foam crumbles, stains bleed or the fabric has started to discolor.
  • You cannot get it dry in a ventilated space.

Keeping a damaged piece that cannot dry can cost more in the end, because the odor can spread to other items and rooms. If you are in doubt, take photos, note the size and age, and then decide.

Document for insurance

List each item with its age, brand and estimated value. Keep receipts if you have them. Photograph the damage and the label. Some policies cover personal belongings damaged by sudden water, subject to deductibles and limits. A renters policy is how tenants usually cover furniture. Ask your insurer what it needs.

Cleaning and drying steps for savable pieces

  1. 1

    Remove loose water

    Blot with towels and press out water. Use a wet vacuum on upholstery only if power and floor are dry.

  2. 2

    Increase airflow

    Aim fans across the piece and run a dehumidifier in the room.

  3. 3

    Flip and lift

    Turn cushions and mattresses so each side dries. Prop pieces up off the floor.

  4. 4

    Check after a day

    Press on the foam and the underside. If it is still wet and smells, let it go.

Do not use high heat to speed things up. Heat can shrink fabric, crack leather and set odors. Sunlight on a dry day helps with surface drying, but thick foam stays wet inside.

What about rugs?

Area rugs are easier than furniture. Take them outside, hang or lay them flat and let them dry, then have them cleaned. Wool and synthetic rugs often recover. Rugs that were soaked with sewage or floodwater are usually discarded, and backing that stayed damp can smell for weeks. Check the underside and the floor beneath the rug, since the floor can stay wet even when the rug dries.

Kids' furniture and bedding

Mattresses and soft furniture that children use deserve extra caution. If the water category is in doubt, replace the item rather than risk a lingering problem. Bedding and clothing can usually be washed on a hot cycle if the water was clean. Anything that touched sewage should be discarded or professionally handled.

Furniture and floors in Los Banos homes

In many homes, a leak that wets a sofa also wets carpet or flooring, and the two need to be dealt with together. Water wicks up from the floor into the base of sofas and beds, so lifting the furniture quickly protects the item and lets the floor dry. Common questions

Can a wet mattress be saved?

A mattress with a small surface spill can sometimes be dried. A mattress soaked through, or wet with contaminated water, is generally discarded.

How long do I have to dry furniture?

The EPA advises drying within 24 to 48 hours. Past that window, odor and mold risk rise sharply.

Will insurance replace my sofa?

Many policies cover belongings damaged by sudden water, subject to limits and deductibles. Ask your insurer and keep photos and a list.
