Frozen pipe damage usually starts when the weather warms, not when it freezes. Ice expands inside a pipe and can split the wall of copper or crack a plastic fitting, and the water only escapes once the ice melts. If a pipe froze and you now hear dripping or see a stain, treat it as an emergency, and call the 24/7 line.

Close the main water valve, leave faucets open so the line can drain, and keep heat on in the house. Do not use an open flame to thaw a pipe, and do not run anything electrical through standing water.

Frozen pipe emergency: what to do when the thaw starts

A pipe that froze is a pipe that may already be cracked. The danger is that it looks fine while frozen, then leaks inside a wall or ceiling after the temperature rises. If you know a pipe froze, shut off the main valve before the thaw and watch the area closely.

The National Weather Service issues freeze warnings for the San Joaquin Valley when hard freezes are expected. Check the forecast before an overnight low, and open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air can reach the pipes.

Where freeze damage tends to show up

Exterior walls

Supply lines in outside walls get the least heat and are the usual place for a split.

Attics and garages

Unheated spaces expose lines and water heater connections to the cold.

Under sinks on outer walls

Cabinet fronts hide a pipe that sits against a cold surface.

Outdoor spigots and irrigation

Hose bibs and sprinkler lines can crack and leak into the wall behind them.

How the restoration works

The crew first confirms the main is closed and finds the split with a moisture meter and thermal camera. Water is extracted, wet insulation is removed, and the wall or ceiling is opened only as far as the moisture reaches.

Drying equipment then runs until the structure reads dry. The plumber repairs the pipe, and the final repair phase replaces any drywall and finish that could not be saved.

Freeze exposure in the Los Banos area

Whether a hard freeze arrives in a given winter varies, and that uncertainty is why freeze damage surprises homeowners. Supply lines in exterior walls, attics and garages get the least heat from the house, so they are the first to freeze. One cold night with a cracked line can release more water than months of small drips.

How often freezes occur here changes from year to year, so check the National Weather Service forecast before a cold night. For any line that has frozen once, ask your plumber to inspect the supply lines on exterior walls first, since added insulation costs less than a rebuilt wall.

What affects frozen pipe damage costs

How long it leaked

A crack found at the first drip is a smaller job than one that ran overnight in a closed wall.

Where the pipe sits

Attic and ceiling splits spread water through insulation and drywall below.

Finishes involved

Painted drywall, wood trim and flooring each need different drying and repair.

Equipment days

Insulated cavities dry slowly, which lengthens the equipment time.

Rebuild scope

Replacing drywall, insulation and paint is its own phase after drying.

A sudden pipe burst from freezing is often treated like other sudden plumbing failures under a homeowners policy, but wording varies, and freeze exclusions or heat requirements can apply to vacant homes. Common questions

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Los Banos?

Yes. Pipes often fail overnight or during the thaw the next morning, so someone picks up whatever the hour, weekends and holidays included. Close the main valve, call, and describe where the water is appearing. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Can a pipe leak days after it froze?

Yes. Ice can hold a crack closed, and the leak starts as the pipe thaws, which can be hours or days later.

Should I thaw a pipe with a heat source?

Use gentle, indirect warmth such as a hair dryer on a low setting, and never an open flame. If the pipe is already leaking, close the main valve and call instead.
