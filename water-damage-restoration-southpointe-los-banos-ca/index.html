New does not mean leak-proof. Water heaters, supply connections, appliance hoses and roofs can all fail in a newer house. If water has appeared, call (831) 271-0459 and we will walk you through the first steps.

Where leaks start in a newer home

Garage water heaters

Many homes keep the tank in the garage, where a leak can reach the slab edge. See water heater leak cleanup.

Slab-level plumbing

A leak under or in a concrete slab shows up as warm floors or damp flooring. See slab leak water damage.

Laundry and kitchen hoses

Fittings loosen and hoses age. See appliance leak damage.

Flooring that gets wet

Laminate and wood floors react to moisture quickly. See hardwood floor repair.

If you smell gas or see sparks

Leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not use switches or lighters.

For a water-only leak, close the main water valve, stay out of standing water near outlets and call us.

Builder paperwork and your records

Some owners still have documents from the builder or a warranty program. Keep them handy and read them for yourself, because we cannot say what any warranty or policy covers. It is also worth keeping the purchase documents for appliances and the water heater.

For an insurance claim, photograph the source and each wet area, keep the failed part and save receipts. We add moisture readings and a written scope. The California Department of Insurance notes flood damage typically needs separate coverage.
