Checking a Los Banos Home for Water Damage Before You Buy

Past water damage is easy to hide and expensive to inherit. A careful look before closing can save thousands.

By the Los Banos Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 10, 2026.

A fresh coat of paint can hide a lot. Before you buy, learn how to read the signs that water has been in a house, and how to ask questions that get honest answers. This guide is for buyers of homes in Los Banos and nearby towns.

It does not replace a licensed home inspection. It gives you a short list of things to notice during a walk-through, so you ask the right follow-up questions.

Signs to look for inside

Stains on ceilings and walls

Brown rings, fresh paint patches and bubbling paint can mean water has been in the wall or ceiling.

Warped or stained floors

Cupped hardwood, buckled laminate and stained carpet edges suggest moisture.

Swollen cabinet bases

Look under sinks and around dishwashers for swollen or stained wood.

Musty smells

A damp smell in a closet or garage can mean hidden moisture. See our structural drying page.

Signs to look for outside

  • Stains on exterior walls under windows and roof edges.
  • Gutters and downspouts that dump water next to the foundation.
  • Soil that slopes toward the house.
  • Rust, staining or efflorescence, a white powder, on concrete.
  • Roof patches, lifted shingles and cracked flashing.

Look under the house and in the garage

If the home has a crawl space, look through the hatch with a flashlight. Check for damp soil, stained insulation, standing water and rusted pipes. A slab home has no crawl space, so look at the garage floor and baseboards for stains and cracks. Our crawl space page and slab leak page describe what each problem looks like.

Questions to ask the seller

QuestionWhy it matters
Has the house had any leaks or water damage?Sellers in California generally must disclose known material facts
Were repairs done by a licensed contractor?Paperwork shows what was fixed and how
Has an insurance claim been filed?Claim history can show repeated problems
Where are the water shutoffs?You will need them on day one
How old are the roof, water heater and plumbing?Age predicts failure

Ask for repair records, inspection reports and contractor invoices. A willing seller can usually produce them.

Bring in the right help

A licensed home inspector looks at the whole house. A moisture check by a restoration crew uses meters and a thermal camera to look behind surfaces. If you see a stain, a smell or a repair patch, ask for a moisture reading before you close. It is easier to negotiate a repair or a credit before the sale than after.

Insurance and flood questions

Ask your agent about coverage before you commit. Standard policies typically exclude flood, according to the California Department of Insurance, and the FEMA flood map for the address shows whether it lies in a mapped flood zone. Ask how the insurer treats past claims on the property.

Reading a repair patch

A repair is not a red flag by itself. Many homes have had a plumbing fix or a roof patch. What matters is the quality of the repair and the record of it. A patch with a visible stain around it, a fresh paint circle on an otherwise aged ceiling or new flooring in only one room can mean a past leak. Ask what happened, when and who did the work.

Look for matching signs in the room below or above. A patched ceiling under a bathroom, for example, suggests a plumbing leak, while a patched ceiling under the roof suggests a roof leak. The location points to the likely source.

Bring a flashlight and a plan

  • Open every cabinet under sinks and check the back panel.
  • Run the faucets and flush the toilets while you listen for leaks.
  • Check the water heater base and the closet around it.
  • Press on the baseboards and the bottom of walls for softness.
  • Photograph anything unusual and ask about it in writing.

A short list makes the visit efficient. Ask the seller or the agent for permission to run the water, and ask for the inspection report if one exists.

Using what you find in negotiation

If you find signs of damage, you have options. You can ask for repairs before closing, a credit toward repairs or more time for a specialist to look. Put requests in writing through your agent. A moisture reading and a written scope give you something concrete to point to.

If the damage is large or the cause is unknown, you may decide to walk away. That is a valid choice. Better to know before you own it.

Common questions

Do sellers have to disclose past water damage?

California generally requires sellers to disclose known material facts about a property. Ask your agent or an attorney how that applies to your purchase.

Can a moisture reading find hidden damage?

Meters and thermal cameras can show moisture behind surfaces, though they do not replace a full inspection.

Should I buy a house that had a leak?

Many homes have had leaks. What matters is whether the source was fixed, the structure dried and the repairs documented.
